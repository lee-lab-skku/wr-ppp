\begin{reportabstract}

Adding a mild calibration penalty improved macro F1 by 2.3 percentage
points while reducing calibration error. The gain was consistent in
four of five random seeds, but the rarest class remains unstable. Next
week's work will therefore focus on label quality and seed sensitivity.

\end{reportabstract}

\begin{pppbox}{Progress}

\ReportSubsection{Moderate Penalty Improves F1 and Calibration}

A moderate calibration penalty improved macro F1 by 2.3 percentage points
and reduced ECE from 8.7 to 5.1 across five seeds
(\cref{tab:main-results}). Data splits and preprocessing were held fixed.

\ReportTable
    {L C C C}
    {
        \toprule
        Method & Macro F1 $\uparrow$ & ECE $\downarrow$ & Worst-class F1 $\uparrow$ \\
        \midrule
        Baseline & 71.4 & 8.7 & 48.2 \\
        Calibration penalty & \textbf{73.7} & 5.1 & \textbf{52.6} \\
        Strong penalty & 72.8 & \textbf{3.9} & 49.7 \\
        \bottomrule
    }
    {Illustrative validation results averaged over five random seeds.}
    {tab:main-results}

The moderate penalty therefore offers the best observed balance for
follow-up validation. The stronger penalty minimized ECE but lost part
of the rare-class gain, so lower ECE alone does not establish a better model.


\ReportSubsection{Penalty Strength Limits the Gain}

The sweep suggests that calibration strength must remain modest:
$\lambda=0.05$ improved calibration without reducing accuracy, whereas
larger values produced under-confident predictions. The tested loss was

\[
    \mathcal{L}
    = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}
    + \frac{\lambda}{N}
      \sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\lVert\mathbf{p}_i-\mathbf{y}_i\right\rVert_2^2.
\]

The effect was strongest near class boundaries, motivating a label audit
before attributing the rare-class gain to the loss formulation.

\end{pppbox}

\begin{pppbox}{Problems}

\ReportSubsection{Rare-Class Instability}

Worst-class F1 still varied by 7.8 points across seeds, so the apparent
gain is not yet strong enough to support a general conclusion.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Working hypothesis:} a small cluster of inconsistently
          labeled samples changes the decision boundary between splits.
    \item \textbf{Attempts and evidence:} class weighting reduced mean
          performance, while manual inspection found three ambiguous samples.
    \item \textbf{Next response:} audit the rare-class labels and repeat the
          comparison with stratified seeds and confidence intervals.
\end{itemize}


\ReportSubsection{Primary Metric Requires a Decision}

The primary metric remains undecided: macro F1 measures class balance,
whereas ECE measures calibration. Advisor feedback on the deployment
priority will determine the main claim; both metrics will remain reported.

\end{pppbox}
